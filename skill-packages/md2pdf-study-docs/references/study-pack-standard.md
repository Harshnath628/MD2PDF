# Harsh's study-pack standard

Agreed 2026-09-13. Explicit task instructions override these defaults. These requirements apply to new study material, not retroactively to formatting-only work.

## Purpose and entry paths

The user generally uses these documents standalone and expects comprehensive explanations. Do not assume prior use of academic-prof, guided practicals, or cold mastery tests. The documents may also consolidate those activities when completed. They support regular learning as well as exam preparation; do not impose a 1-2-day deadline unless the current task is exam-focused.

Repetition through multiple question angles is intentional. Retain brief repeated principles where they make an answer understandable. Do not dismiss useful repetition as redundancy or replace explanations with 'already explained'. Reading a solved bank is not proof of independent mastery.

## Two deliverables

For a full study pack, deliver:

1. **Study notes + worked examples:** one document, topic-wise, with each topic's worked examples beside its explanation.
2. **Solved question bank:** one document, topic-wise, with each answer immediately below its question.

Use matching topic names and order across both documents. Do not split worked examples into a third document by default. If the user requests only one deliverable, produce that one. Independent/cold tests are separate optional requests whose answer keys remain separate; do not convert the solved bank into a hidden-answer test.

## Natural progression

Build a prerequisite order from the authorized syllabus and professor sources before drafting. Cover all in-scope topics, including those absent from available PYQs. Preserve course identifiers when reordering so coverage remains traceable.

Each topic connects to prior knowledge, explains why its new idea is needed, introduces necessary terms/notation, develops the idea, and demonstrates it with worked examples. Adapt this sequence to the subject rather than forcing every topic into mathematics and code.

Later topics' questions and answers should reuse earlier concepts where natural. This provides cumulative practice inside the topic-wise organization. Do not require future topics before teaching them. Supply a minimal labeled prerequisite bridge if an essential prerequisite is missing.

Study notes must explain sufficiently for a standalone learner: definitions, assumptions, symbols, mechanisms, and intermediate reasoning. Revision notes may be shorter only when explicitly requested; do not silently substitute compressed revision sheets for study notes.

## Question count and organization

For each full unit bank, provide **at least 100 complete question-answer pairs**. This is a minimum, not a cap. Allocate questions across topics according to breadth, foundational role, meaningful angles, and supported emphasis. Do not force equal allocation or neglect syllabus topics with no PYQs.

The hierarchy is **unit > topic > question type > easy to hard within each type**. Prefer conceptual, numerical, derivation/proof, comparison, then other relevant types such as construction, interpretation, debugging, counterfactual, or application. Omit inapplicable categories. Do not first group the whole unit by difficulty or impose numerical questions on every topic.

Use stable IDs, preferably U1-Q001 through U1-Q100 and beyond, and visible difficulty labels. Count each substantive question with its answer as one pair; do not count subparts as separate questions to inflate the total. Include genuine PYQs in the total. Deduplicate repeated appearances of the same PYQ as one question and record recurrence rather than counting identical copies.

For output constraints, generate in manageable batches but assemble the two complete final documents, preserve numbering, and verify counts. Do not quietly stop at a sample or claim completion before reaching the requested count. If continuation is technically necessary, state the completed IDs and exact remainder.

## Professor-level question design

Design with the judgment of a third-/fourth-year B.Tech computer science professor: clear learning purpose, technically precise wording, sufficient given information, appropriate assumptions, and a defensible answer. Match the course's actual depth; do not make basic questions artificially advanced.

Difficulty should come from reasoning, synthesis, or transfer, not missing information, obscure trivia, or ambiguous wording. Use subject-appropriate work: automata/proofs for TOC, kernels/transforms for DIP, protocol traces for CN, mathematical mechanisms and implementation where relevant for AI/ML.

Revisit concepts from distinct angles: explain, calculate, derive, compare, interpret, identify a failure, predict a change, apply in a fresh context, or combine with previously introduced concepts. Controlled numerical variants can reinforce a mechanism, but the bank must not consist mainly of superficial paraphrases or value substitutions.

## Answer depth

- **Easy:** a short-answer response with a brief explanation. Still state necessary conditions or essential calculation steps.
- **Medium:** explain the method and intermediate reasoning, with a worked calculation or example where useful.
- **Hard:** provide fuller derivation, proof, construction, or multi-step analysis, addressing relevant assumptions and interpretation.

Do not use arbitrary word counts. Scale explanation to reasoning demands, not merely answer length. Define new symbols and show important substitutions, matrix dimensions, intermediate matrices, and operations. Give enough reasoning to understand each answer without another chat; concise reminders of prior concepts are welcome. Cross-references may add depth but must not replace the essential answer.

Verify numerical results, formula conditions, proof validity, construction behavior, and code logic where relevant. Clearly flag unreadable or conflicting source information rather than guessing.

## PYQs and external B.Tech evidence

Analyze supplied PYQs for question types, depth, marks when available, reasoning steps, wording, recurring concepts, and combinations. Report patterns over the actual sample; do not infer year-on-year trends from a single paper or invent probabilities.

For a new full question bank, also research publicly available papers or assignments from other B.Tech colleges, using official institutional or university sources where available. Compare relevant courses and level, not merely similarly named subjects. Record institution, course, year when known, and direct source link. Do not claim a general trend from one external example. If browsing or papers are unavailable, disclose that and proceed from available course evidence without invented external findings.

External research broadens question design but cannot expand the user's syllabus. Filter external questions through the authorized scope and preserve the home professor's notation and conventions. Distinguish:

- Actual supplied PYQ, with known paper/year/question reference.
- External question or adaptation, with source attribution.
- Generated question inspired by an observed pattern.
- Generated coverage or transfer question beyond observed patterns.

Respect applicable source-use limits: prefer original questions or clearly labeled adaptations over copying large portions of external copyrighted banks. Do not fabricate exam years, marks, teacher endorsements, or source references.

**PYQs are stepping stones, never walls.** Include easier foundations, comparable questions, and harder/unseen in-scope applications. The user's illustrative '+/-20%' is not a numerical cap, quota, or measurable difficulty formula. Cover concepts never examined before and combine earlier topics where useful. Do not limit the bank to historical question templates.

State priority evidence briefly: teacher emphasis, observed PYQ recurrence, prerequisite importance, or general academic judgment. Separate evidence from forecast; no guaranteed exam predictions.

## Completion checks

Before delivering, verify:

- Both requested documents follow the same coherent topic progression.
- Every authorized syllabus topic is covered or an explicit source gap is disclosed.
- Each full unit bank has at least 100 substantive, answered, consistently numbered questions.
- Question-type groups precede difficulty ordering; answer depth scales appropriately.
- Questions revisit meaningful angles and later topics reinforce earlier concepts.
- Source labels distinguish actual papers, adaptations, and original generation.
- Mathematical/proof/construction answers are checked at the appropriate level.
- Markdown follows the combined formatting references; export claims reflect actual verification.

Report counts per unit and any material omissions or unavailable evidence. Do not add dashboards, mocks, error-log files, or website changes unless requested.
